# Nava Bhasha — The Nine Tongues League

*A 30-minute-a-day language mastery system for Raagavan, built as a game.*

Nine tracks, nine grahas. Each language sits on the board as a graha with a level, a points balance, and a decay clock. You play against one opponent — the Forgetting Curve — and the game theory is the strategy guide.

## The daily session — 30 minutes, three blocks

| Block | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Anchor** | 15 | Deep work on the day's anchor language: new material — a lesson, a phrase set, listening + shadowing. New ground is only ever broken here. |
| **Satellites** | 10 (2 × 5) | Review drills in two *other* languages. Which two is decided by the morning auction (below), not by mood. |
| **Script sprint** | 5 | One script, reading + writing only. Scripts get their own slot because they decay differently from speech. |

**Script sprint rotation** (repeating 4-day cycle): Tamil → Devanagari → Telugu → Malayalam → repeat.

**Eclipse days.** Some days will be chaos — the baby is coming in January, and life will not ask permission. An eclipse day is a declared 10-minute minimum session (anchor only, shortened). It keeps the streak alive at reduced points. Declaring an eclipse costs nothing; *skipping silently* is the only real loss. Rahu and Ketu approve.

## The game — six mechanics, all game theory

### 1. The Forgetting Curve is the adversary (minimax)
Memory decays predictably; every day you don't touch a language, it takes points off your retention. This is a two-player game: the Curve "attacks" wherever your retention is weakest, so your optimal strategy is **minimax** — allocate effort to minimise your maximum loss. Translation: study what is closest to being forgotten, not what is most fun. Favourites get anchor days; the auction protects everything else.

### 2. The morning auction (mechanism design)
Each morning, every language "bids" for the two satellite slots with its **decay debt** — days since last review, weighted by level. Highest debts win the slots. The mechanism is designed so that **honest self-reporting is the optimal strategy**: the bids come from your own tracking, and inflating a bid only steals review time from a language that needs it more — you would only be lying to yourself at your own expense. Truth-telling is the dominant strategy; the auction just runs it.

### 3. The weekly stake (Ulysses contract + loss aversion)
Every Sunday you stake **150 points** on the coming week. Each completed day pays back 30; a missed day burns its 30. Losses hurt roughly twice as much as equivalent gains feel good — that asymmetry is the engine. You are Ulysses tying himself to the mast *before* the sirens (a tired Tuesday evening) start singing. The stake is set when you're strong, and it holds you when you're not.

### 4. The boss fight (weakest-link test)
Sunday, 10 minutes: a short conversation or comprehension test in the week's weakest language — lowest level, highest decay debt. The adversary picks the battlefield, so the rational move all week is to shore up the weakest link before Sunday exposes it. Win = level-appropriate score (Muse grades); a win pays 100 points and can trigger a level-up check. A loss pays 25 for showing up and writes the rematch into next week's satellites.

### 5. The repeated game (cooperation bonus)
A streak is a repeated game against your future self: cooperate today and tomorrow's player inherits points, momentum, and a multiplier; defect and he inherits a restart. In repeated games, cooperation is sustainable in a way it never is one-shot — that's the folk theorem doing your motivation for you. Optional two-player mode: if Prerna plays any track, shared streak days pay both of you a 25% bonus. Defection by one doesn't burn the other — that's deliberate; guilt is a bad mechanic.

### 6. Reward cards (variable-ratio reinforcement)
After every session, Muse deals one card from the reward deck — a phrase worth knowing, a song, a scene, a meme, a piece of graha lore, occasionally a wild card (double points next session, or an anchor override). You never know which is coming. Variable-ratio schedules are the most durable reinforcement pattern known; slot machines use them for evil, we use them for Telugu.

## Levels — same ladder on every track

| Level | Name | Milestone (plain version) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Unplaced | Not yet honestly assessed. Week 1 fixes this. |
| 1 | Survival | Greetings, numbers, politeness, ~100 core words; script basics where one exists. |
| 2 | Travel-ready | Directions, food, transport, money, small problems — handled without a phone. |
| 3 | Conversational | A 10-minute conversation; narrate yesterday and tomorrow. |
| 4 | Fluent reader | Read a children's book or a news page unaided. For Tamil: formal essays and literature. |
| 5 | Mastery track | Nuance: humour, argument, register-switching. Open-ended by design. |

## Points economy

- Study minute = **10 points** to that language (full session = 300, split by block).
- Boss fight: win **+100**, show up **+25**.
- Weekly stake: 150 in, 30 back per completed day.
- Streak multiplier: ×1.1 at 7 days, ×1.25 at 30 days (applied to session points).
- Spend points on: anchor override (200), boss-fight postponement (300, once a month), an immersion day unlock (1,000 — a half-day themed deep-dive Muse builds for you).

## Win conditions — the first 90 days

1. All nine tracks placed by end of Week 1.
2. **Spanish at Level 2 (travel-ready)** by Day 90.
3. All four scripts readable at Level 1 — a shop sign, a menu, a bus board.
4. Tamil: one short formal text read end-to-end, unaided.
5. **75+ sessions logged** out of ~90.
6. At least one boss fight won on every track.
7. No track left at Level 0 after Day 30.

## Week 1 — placement week

Small, concrete, honest. Every day is a normal 30-minute session; the anchor block is a placement probe, not a lesson. "I don't know" is a valid and valuable answer all week — a false placement poisons the auction for months.

- **Day 1 (Mon) — Setup + Hindi.** Meet the nine grahas; set the first stake (Week 1 is stake-free — placement week is exempt). Hindi probe: read 5 Devanagari words, greet, count to 20, 5 survival phrases. First script sprint (Tamil), first reward card.
- **Day 2 (Tue) — Telugu.** Probe: script recognition, greetings, 5 phrases, one listening snippet. Script sprint: Devanagari.
- **Day 3 (Wed) — Spanish.** Retention check on Phrase Set 1 from your travel goal — what survived, what's gone. That *is* the placement. Script sprint: Telugu.
- **Day 4 (Thu) — Malayalam.** Probe: script recognition (it will look like a beautiful knot — that's fine), kinship and home words, greetings. Script sprint: Malayalam.
- **Day 5 (Fri) — German.** Probe: greetings, numbers, 5 phrases, one listening snippet. Script sprint: Tamil.
- **Day 6 (Sat) — Tamil, formal.** Read one short formal paragraph aloud; write 5 sentences by hand. Placement here is about the *formal* register — spoken Tamil doesn't count toward it. Script sprint: Devanagari.
- **Day 7 (Sun) — English lab + first boss fight.** Register probe: rewrite 3 sentences US → UK and back; spot-the-idiom round. Then the first boss fight in the week's weakest track. Muse sets all nine levels in the state file, and the first real stake is placed for Week 2.

## What Muse does

- **Daily:** builds the day's session card — anchor lesson, the two satellite drills the auction selected, the script sprint — and deals the reward card after.
- **Weekly (Sunday):** runs and grades the boss fight, settles the stake, checks level-up conditions, sets next week's stake.
- **Always:** keeps the scoreboard honest in the companion state file (`state.md`, same folder) — levels, points, streak, decay debts. The scoreboard is the game; if it isn't written there, it didn't happen.
